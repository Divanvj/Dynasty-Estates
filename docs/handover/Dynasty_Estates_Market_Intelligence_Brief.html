01Executive summary

A two-portal market that monetises far more than listings.

Property24 and Private Property dominate South African property search. Both earn from subscriptions, promotions, data and software, not only from listing fees. Dynasty’s opening: affordable distribution, measurable performance, and an AI layer that improves results rather than just reporting them.

01

A two-horse market

Property24 leads (~7.33m visits) with Private Property second (~2.37m). The 2021 split was ~68% vs ~23% of traffic, so the market has barely moved.

02

One listing, many revenue events

Subscription, then promotion, data and software. Property24’s sale matrix alone spans R656–R84,256 a month.

03

The AI gap is real

Neither portal publicly documents an autonomous agent that manages listings, budgets and leads. Analytics exists; agentic workflows don’t.

04

Revenue before scale

1,000 paying customers ≈ R1.09m / month in the base model. Dynasty doesn’t need Property24-sized inventory to be a business.

05

Inventory comes from offices

2,000 offices × 100 listings = 200,000 properties. Franchise and feed integrations beat one-by-one seller acquisition.

Six ways Dynasty can be better

Claim what is verifiably different. Avoid saying incumbents have “no analytics” or “no AI”.

01 · PRICING

Transparent, published plans

Agents see exactly what they pay and what they get. No lead × price matrix and no “price on enquiry”.

02 · FLEXIBILITY

Durations that match intent

7-day to 180-day private packages and 24-hour boosts turn a R2,299 decision into a R149 one.

03 · ACCOUNTABILITY

Pay for exposure that performs

A performance-guarantee wallet credits spend back when qualified engagement falls short.

04 · INSIGHT

Full-funnel Intelligence Console

From impression to sold or let, with cost per verified lead, listing health and market benchmarks.

05 · AGENTIC AI

Ask Dynasty & the Performance Wallet

Natural-language answers, then approved actions inside a hard budget, with every outcome attributed.

06 · NEUTRALITY

Integration-first, vendor-neutral

PropCtrl sits inside Property24’s own product stack. Dynasty can be the neutral partner that independent listing engines want to feed.

The autonomous Performance Wallet

The feature portals don’t usually have: subscriptions, promoted ranking and AI combined into one budget-capped loop.

Fund

An agency places R2,000 / month into its Dynasty Performance Wallet.

Evaluate

Every listing is scored on impressions, enquiries, local demand, age and price competitiveness.

Recommend

Boost, fix the price, rewrite the copy, or time the push. Each comes with expected effect and cost.

Authorise

Approval mode first. Later, autopilot for approved action types, never above the hard cap.

Attribute

Leads and viewings are traced back to each action and explained to the agent and seller.

13Risks & next steps

Decisions for management.

Approve a focused pilot with a defined segment, an inventory-quality threshold, a measurable qualified-lead outcome and a capped acquisition budget.

Research + recommendations

Approve a focused pilot

Two or three metros or suburbs, one agency segment and a capped acquisition budget. Density beats national thinness.

Owner · Management

Validate pricing in the field

Run 20–30 agency interviews and collect written incumbent quotes before locking launch prices.

Owner · Commercial

Sign the first feed partner

Integrate one independent listing engine; prove deduplication, freshness and withdrawal sync.

Owner · Product & engineering

Instrument the funnel on day one

Impression to verified lead to viewing to outcome, with bot filtering, so that AI decisions rest on trustworthy data.

Owner · Engineering & data

Define a “qualified lead”

The threshold behind the performance guarantee and pay-per-verified-lead. Agree it before selling either.

Owner · Product

Build the cost side of the model

CAC, onboarding, support, data licensing, AI inference, hosting, payment fees and bad debt, so we can see margin and not just billings.

Owner · Finance

Evidence gaps & risks

  • No audited national inventory or billable-client census exists for either portal.
  • Incumbent revenue and payer figures are low-confidence models, not disclosures.
  • Competitor negotiated rates, discounts and VAT treatment are not normalised.
  • Public documentation cannot prove a competitor lacks a capability.
  • Buyer traffic is the hardest asset to build; listings without demand don’t retain agents.
  • Incumbent response risk: price cuts or bundled AI features once Dynasty gains traction.

Questions to resolve

  • Which first suburbs and agency segment can we serve densely?
  • What do agency interviews and current quotes say agents will pay?
  • Can feed partners grant rights and remove stale listings quickly?
  • What qualified-lead threshold justifies a paid promotion?
  • What are contribution margin, payback and retention by account type?
  • Do we launch with a free tier, an inclusion programme, or both?
14Sources & method

Where the numbers come from.

Built from the 19-page research paper “Dynasty Estates: South African Property Portal, Monetisation and Real-Estate Intelligence Research” (research snapshot, 18 October 2026). Primary references cited in the paper are below; they may have changed since.

Paying customer

An agent or agency account billed in the month, or a private lister who bought a package that month. Not a browsing buyer.

Gross billings

Amounts billed, excluding VAT, before payment processing, commissions, marketing, hosting, AI inference and staff costs. Not revenue recognition or profit.

Annualised

One month × 12, not a realised year or contracted ARR. Incumbent models exclude broader advertising, referrals and group data licensing.
